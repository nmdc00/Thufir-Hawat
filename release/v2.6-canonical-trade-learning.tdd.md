**Status:** Proposed

production database at `f8ec9bd`)  
**Supersedes where inconsistent:** v2.1 learning-loop TDD, v2.2 adaptive-learning TDD,

## Executive Decision

Thufir has learning components, but production does not yet have one trustworthy learning loop.
The system records entry journals, predictions, reduce events, execution-quality cases, canonical
closes, reflections, signal-weight updates, and policies. Those records are produced by overlapping
paths with different context and PnL semantics.

v2.6 makes one finalized trade lifecycle the only admissible unit of execution learning:

```text
candidate -> entry intent -> execution -> lifecycle events -> canonical trade_close
                                                        |             |
                                                        |             +-> prediction resolution
                                                        |             +-> learning projections
                                                        |             +-> policy evidence
                                                        |             +-> counterfactual replay
                                                        +-> no policy mutation before finalization
```

Learning to say no can improve returns only when rejected opportunities are measured against what
would have happened and when accepted and rejected candidates are deduplicated. A higher rejection
rate is not itself evidence of learning. The optimization target is out-of-sample net expectancy in
risk units, subject to drawdown and data-quality constraints.

### Current runtime loop (as-is)

```text
entry gate ──(inline, unversioned: sharpe filter + calibration segment + decision-quality segment,
              each recomputed per call from the latest 200-500 journals, ~97% blocked rows)
    └─> execution ──(reduce-only call writes one execution_quality case per exit slice,
                     from close-local context; action recorded as sell/reduceOnly)
            └─> close event ──(net PnL recomputed from exit fee only)
                    └─> finalizer ──(context scavenged from closePayload ?? entryPayload ??
                                     latest learning case; repairs held in memory, never persisted;
                                     trade_closes keyed on close_event_id, not lifecycle;
                                     five persistence writes, no transaction)
                            └─> policy materialization ──(rate triggers on >=3 slice-samples,
                                                          activated immediately; the "promotion
                                                          event" is an audit row for a change
                                                          that is already live)
prediction resolution ──> signal-weight gradient over scores synthesized from the decision's own
                          confidence ──> weights fetched next scan ──> stored as metadata only
```

### Why Existing Tests Missed This

Current tests prove isolated surfaces. For example, policy tests manually create complete
`execution_quality` rows, finalizer tests manually construct close events, and live/paper parity
compares close journals and prediction metadata. They do not assert equality across the paper book,
close event, canonical close, prediction, and learning case after one real lifecycle. Some tests also
encode the incorrect behavior, including `action.reduceOnly === true` and direction score derived
from `thesisCorrect`.

## Goals

1. Make one lifecycle-level record authoritative for accounting, context, labels, and eligibility.
2. Generate one independent execution-learning sample per completed position lifecycle.
3. Separate market-direction outcome, process compliance, thesis invalidation, and profitability.
4. Train and activate policies on clean, eligible, net-of-cost outcomes using uncertainty-aware
   expectancy rather than rejection count, raw win rate, or asset quantity.
5. Measure rejected decisions with deduplicated shadow lifecycles before claiming that saying no is
   improving returns.
6. Prove that promoted learning changes a later autonomous decision through the production entrypoint.

## Non-goals

- Enabling live trading or changing exchange execution behavior.
- Allowing an LLM reflection to overwrite deterministic facts.
- Treating historical rows with missing provenance as clean training data.
- Automatically promoting a policy from one trade or from in-sample performance.
- Maintaining two adaptive-policy implementations. The canonical consumer remains
  `src/core/autonomy_policy.ts`; disconnected alternatives are removed or reduced to pure helpers.

## Canonical Data Model

### 1. `trade_entry_contexts` - immutable decision snapshot

Create one row after the entry gate returns an allowed decision and before the first execution call.
No transaction is ever held across an exchange call. The row moves through an explicit state
machine, with each transition its own short transaction:

```text
prepared -> execution_submitted -> opened
                     └-----------> execution_failed
```

1. Transaction A (before execution): insert the context in `prepared` with a unique
   `execution_idempotency_key`, commit.
2. Mark `execution_submitted` immediately before the exchange call.
3. Transaction B (after the execution result): attach `trade_id` and fill identity and mark
   `opened`, or mark `execution_failed` with the failure payload.

Crash recovery: on startup and on a recovery sweep, any context stuck in `prepared` older than the
submission timeout is marked `execution_failed(abandoned_before_submit)`. Any context stuck in
`execution_submitted` is reconciled against the execution authority using its
`execution_idempotency_key` — if a matching order/fill exists it is completed to `opened`, otherwise
it is marked `execution_failed(no_execution_found)` after the exchange's declared visibility window.
`execution_failed` contexts are terminal, keep their candidate linkage for rejection/failure
accounting, and never acquire a lifecycle.

Decision-snapshot columns are immutable after `prepared`; the only mutable fields are the state
machine fields (`status`, `trade_id`, `lifecycle_id`, fill identity, failure payload, timestamps).

Required columns:

```text
id, lifecycle_id, trade_id, candidate_id, prediction_id
status, execution_idempotency_key, submitted_at, opened_at, failed_at, failure_payload
symbol, symbol_class, execution_mode, entry_side
requested_size, effective_size, leverage, entry_notional_usd
risk_budget_usd, risk_fraction, expected_edge
signal_class, trigger_reason, entry_trigger, strategy_source, session_tag
market_regime, volatility_bucket, liquidity_bucket, trade_archetype
invalidation_type, invalidation_price, thesis_expires_at, time_stop_at
policy_version, active_adjustment_ids, source_authority
decision_payload, context_schema_version, created_at
```

Required means the writer must supply a value or an explicit `unknown`/`not_applicable` enum where
the concept is categorical. Missing required context is a write failure before execution, not a null
silently manufactured at close.

`candidate_id` is stable across retries in the same scan/cooldown window. It links accepted and
rejected candidate evidence without counting repeated gate attempts as independent observations.
It is a canonical fingerprint, implemented exactly once in a shared helper (never re-derived
ad hoc), over:

```text
strategy_source, symbol, entry_side, signal_class, trigger_reason,
proposal identity (thesis/invalidation identity where present),
scan_id, normalized decision inputs,
floor(scan_time_ms / cooldown_window_ms)
```

Time-bucket collisions between genuinely different proposals are prevented by the proposal-identity
and normalized-decision-input components, not by the time bucket alone. Invariant 11 is tested
against this single implementation.

### 1a. Lifecycle identity

`lifecycle_id` is the uniqueness boundary for closes, samples, and evidence, so its generation and
retry semantics are binding:

- A lifecycle is created when an entry context reaches `opened` and the position transitions from
  flat to non-flat for `(symbol, execution_mode)`. Its ID is derived from the opening trade
  (`perp:<symbol>:<trade_id>`), never re-minted for the same opened trade.
- A rejected or failed execution after context creation gets no lifecycle (the context ends
  `execution_failed`).
- Partial entry fills belong to the same lifecycle; the entry context records requested vs
  effective size, and fill identity accumulates on the same lifecycle.
- Scale-ins (adding to an existing non-flat position) join the EXISTING lifecycle as additional
  entry fills with their own entry contexts linked by `lifecycle_id`; they do not open a second
  lifecycle. The first context on the lifecycle is primary for decision-snapshot purposes; the
  learning sample records aggregate entry economics.
- A position flip (reduce through zero to the opposite side) terminates the old lifecycle at the
  zero crossing and opens a new lifecycle for the residual opposite-side exposure; one execution
  may therefore produce a terminal event for one lifecycle and an opening fill for another.
- Simultaneous close requests are serialized by the finalizer claim (`BEGIN IMMEDIATE` +
  idempotency key); the loser of the race must observe the winner's terminal event and not mint a
  second terminal event.
- Late or duplicated exchange fills for a finalized lifecycle never mutate the canonical close in
  place: they are recorded as post-finalization reconciliation deltas that flip the close to
  `quarantined(late_fill_mismatch)` when they break reconciliation, triggering deterministic
  re-finalization under a new accounting run — same lifecycle, versioned re-evidence, never a
  second sample.

### 2. `trade_close_events` - append-only execution facts

Keep this table as the append-only record of partial and final reductions. Add:

```text
entry_context_id, event_sequence, fill_ids_payload
gross_realized_pnl_usd, entry_fees_allocated_usd, close_fees_usd, funding_usd
canonical_net_pnl_usd, accounting_schema_version
```

The executor must call one shared `buildPerpCloseAccounting(...)` function and pass its returned
object unchanged to the event writer, prediction resolver, and response metadata. No writer may
recompute net PnL from a subset of fees.

For each event:

```text
canonical_net_pnl_usd = gross_realized_pnl_usd
                        - entry_fees_allocated_usd
                        - close_fees_usd
                        - funding_usd
```

Entry fees are allocated proportionally across partial reductions. The sum allocated over the
lifecycle must equal the actual entry fees, with the final event absorbing floating-point residue.

Binding accounting definitions:

- **Representation.** All canonical USD amounts are computed internally in integer micro-USD
  (1e-6 USD) with half-even rounding at ingestion, and persisted as their exact decimal value.
  "Exact" in this document means exact in micro-USD; the `1e-8` reconciliation tolerance applies
  only when comparing against float-derived legacy values and external authorities.
- **Funding sign.** `funding_usd` is a signed net cash flow in USD over the allocation window:
  positive means the position paid funding (reduces net PnL, consistent with the subtraction in the
  formula), negative means it received funding.
- **Multiple entry fills / scale-ins.** Entry fees are the sum over all entry fills on the
  lifecycle; allocation to a reduction is proportional to `size_reduced / total_opened_size` using
  the size-weighted aggregate entry economics, recomputed as scale-ins land. A reduction that
  precedes a later scale-in allocates only against fills that existed at its event sequence.
- **Late or disagreeing live fills.** The finalizer does not guess: if the fill authority has not
  converged when finalization is attempted, the job retries on its backoff schedule up to a
  declared window; past the window, the close finalizes as `quarantined(fill_authority_pending)`.
  Fills that arrive after finalization follow the late-fill rule in §1a.

Funding: no funding-payment capture exists today — `funding_paid_usd` is hardcoded to zero at the
write site and neither the paper book nor the exchange client observes account funding payments.
Accounting schema v1 therefore sets `funding_usd = 0` and `funding_supported = false`. Eligibility
does not require funding until a capture mechanism ships under a new accounting schema version. A
nonzero `funding_usd` under `funding_supported = false` is a reconciliation failure, and no writer
may silently infer funding from price drift.

### 3. `trade_closes` - canonical completed lifecycle

Retain `trade_closes` as the authoritative finalized outcome and add:

```text
entry_context_id, prediction_id, accounting_schema_version, label_schema_version
entry_fees_usd, close_fees_usd, funding_usd, net_r
direction_outcome, process_compliant, profitable_after_costs
learning_eligibility, ineligibility_reasons, source_data_cutoff
evidence_hash, policy_version_at_entry
```

There is exactly one row per `lifecycle_id`, enforced by a unique index. `close_event_id` identifies
the terminal event but is not the lifecycle uniqueness key.

The close finalizer aggregates all lifecycle events and fills. It does not trust a close-local
learning case. It joins `trade_entry_contexts`, calculates labels from deterministic facts, writes
the canonical close, and then materializes all projections in one database transaction.

### 4. Learning projections

`learning_cases` remains a read model, not a source of truth. A completed eligible close produces:

- one `execution_quality` case linked by `trade_close_id` and `lifecycle_id`;
- one prediction resolution when a prediction exists;
- zero or one thesis-quality case depending on whether the required path/horizon evidence exists;
- zero or more counterfactual results, all linked to the same close and price-path version.

Partial reductions may produce `exit_slice` diagnostics, but never independent policy sample weight.
Rebuilding projections from `trade_closes` must be deterministic and idempotent.

## Label Semantics

The following labels are independent:

| Label | Meaning | Derivation |
|---|---|---|
| `profitable_after_costs` | The lifecycle made money | `net_realized_pnl_usd > 0` |
| `direction_outcome` | The predicted direction was correct over the declared horizon | Entry side compared with the versioned price path/horizon; `correct`, `incorrect`, or `unresolved` |
| `thesis_invalidation_hit` | The explicit invalidation condition occurred | Exit contract and observed path |
| `process_compliant` | Entry, sizing, and exit followed the policy active at entry | Deterministic policy trace, independent of PnL: effective size and leverage within gate-approved values, exit consistent with the declared invalidation/time-stop contract, and no post-entry mutation of that contract |
| `net_r` | Net outcome in risk units | `canonical_net_pnl_usd / risk_budget_usd` from the entry context; when the risk budget or invalidation is absent, `net_r` is null and the close is ineligible as policy evidence |
| `timing_score` | Entry quality relative to favorable/adverse excursion | Versioned MAE/MFE calculation |
| `sizing_score` | Risk taken relative to approved risk budget | Effective risk USD divided by approved risk USD, never raw asset size |
| `exit_score` | Fraction of attainable risk-adjusted move captured, with contract compliance | Versioned path calculation |

A planned stop can therefore be `process_compliant=true`, `direction_outcome=incorrect`, and
`profitable_after_costs=false`. This is expected and must not be collapsed into `thesisCorrect=true`.
Legacy `thesis_correct` remains readable during migration but is not policy evidence under label
schema v2.

Label schema v2 also governs the finalizer's secondary projections. Deterministic trade reflections
and `regret_learning_cases` are currently derived from the same conflated `thesisCorrect` and
close-local `capturedR`; both carry policy-evidence payloads. Under v2.6 they are rebuilt from the
canonical close's labels inside the finalizer transaction, or they are not written at all.

## Eligibility and Historical Data

Introduce one shared `LearningEligibility` service used by policy materialization, adaptive edge,
prediction baselines, signal-weight training, counterfactual evaluation, and dashboards.

A close is eligible only when:

1. it is newer than `learning.cleanDataCutoff`;
2. entry context, lifecycle ID, fills, and terminal close are linked;
3. the mode-appropriate authority and canonical PnL reconcile within `1e-8` USD or an
   exchange-specific declared precision — the paper-book realized delta in paper mode, the
   aggregated fill authority in live mode;
4. required label inputs are present;
5. accounting and label schema versions are supported;
6. it is not a bootstrap, manual repair, test probe, or duplicate.

Positions already open when v2.6 deploys have no entry context and finalize as bootstrap
lifecycles (`perp:<symbol>:bootstrap:*`). They are quarantined under rule 6, permanently; nobody
reconstructs their context at close time — that is the same anti-pattern this document removes.

Historical rows are backfilled only when these invariants can be reconstructed deterministically.
Everything else is marked `quarantined` with machine-readable reasons. Backfill must never infer a
missing regime, edge, or leverage from a later close. Given the measured state of production data
(leverage absent in 425 of 426 execution cases, regime absent in 335), the expected backfill
outcome is near-total quarantine: learning restarts from the v2.6 cutoff, and a high quarantine
rate is the correct result of migration, not a migration bug.

Resolved evidence queries filter eligibility in SQL before applying a limit. Blocked journal rows
cannot crowd completed outcomes out of a training window.

## Policy Learning

### Objective

For each hierarchical cohort, calculate:

```text
net_expectancy_r = sum(net_r * sample_weight) / sum(sample_weight)
uncertainty      = empirical/Bayesian interval around net_expectancy_r
drawdown_cost    = cohort downside and tail-loss penalty
policy_utility   = net_expectancy_r - uncertainty_penalty - drawdown_cost
```

Cohorts back off from specific to broad only as evidence requires:

```text
signal + direction + regime + volatility + liquidity + session + strategy
signal + direction + regime + volatility
signal + direction + regime
signal + direction
global
```

The selected level and effective sample size are persisted. This prevents both one-sample exact
segments and destructive global aggregation.

### Candidate, shadow, promotion

New policies and signal weights begin as versioned challengers. A challenger has no runtime effect
until it passes:

- minimum effective sample size;
- forward-only or walk-forward evaluation;
- improved net expectancy with a configured confidence margin;
- no unacceptable drawdown or rejection-rate regression;
- paper shadow period;
- explicit promotion event.

`autonomy_policy.evaluateGlobalTradeGate` reads only the active promoted version. Every later decision
records the policy version, adjustment IDs, requested size, effective size, and whether adaptation
changed the outcome. This trace is the proof that learning is wired.

Signal weights follow the same champion/challenger contract. Promoted weights must participate in
the probability or ranking calculation; metadata-only snapshots do not count as runtime learning.

Weight training has a hard prerequisite: independent signal measurements. Today every stored signal
score is synthesized from the decision's own confidence (`buildOriginatorSignalScores`,
`buildDiscoverySignalScores`), so gradient updates learn only the correlation of the LLM's
confidence with outcomes, refracted through trade-type constants. Promoting such weights into the
probability calculation would create a confidence feedback loop wrapped in a compliant pipeline.
Until entry-time capture of genuinely independent technical, news, and on-chain measurements ships,
weight training is frozen: no weight challenger may be created from confidence-derived scores, and
the existing gradient-update path is disabled alongside the other disconnected learners.

## Learning to Say No

Rejected candidates get a deduplicated `candidate_lifecycles` row with the same context schema as an
accepted entry. Only economically meaningful gate decisions create shadow observations; cooldown,
duplicate-book, malformed-input, and infrastructure retries are operational suppressions, not model
rejections.

For eligible model rejections, a shadow evaluator records the price path until the original
invalidation or time stop and computes hypothetical net R using the same fee/slippage model. Policy
evaluation compares:

- accepted-trade net expectancy;
- rejected-candidate shadow expectancy;
- avoided-loss R;
- missed-profit R;
- opportunity cost and change in drawdown;
- deduplicated acceptance rate.

A policy becomes better at saying no only when avoided losses exceed missed gains out of sample.

## Counterfactuals

Replace fixed multipliers of realized PnL with replay over persisted price observations. Supported
counterfactuals initially are no trade, approved size cap, approved leverage cap, original
invalidation, and original time stop. Each stores input path version, fee model version, result
payload, and reproducibility hash. A counterfactual without a result is `pending` or `failed`, never
silently counted as evidence.

## Runtime Ownership and Wiring

| Stage | Canonical owner | Required output |
|---|---|---|
| Candidate deduplication | autonomous scan/originator boundary | `candidate_id` |
| Gate decision | `src/core/autonomy_policy.ts` | versioned policy trace |
| Entry snapshot | new `src/memory/trade_entry_contexts.ts` | immutable entry context |
| Execution accounting | extracted `src/core/perp_close_accounting.ts` | one canonical accounting object |
| Partial/full event persistence | `src/memory/close_trade_finalizer.ts` | append-only event |
| Lifecycle finalization | `src/core/close_trade_finalizer.ts` | one canonical `trade_closes` row and projections |
| Eligibility | new `src/core/learning_eligibility.ts` | eligible/quarantined result |
| Policy training | refactored `src/core/trade_policy_materialization.ts` | inactive challenger |
| Runtime consumption | `src/core/autonomy_policy.ts` | promoted policy effect and trace |
| Price-path capture | new recorder component (shared by direction labels, shadow outcomes, counterfactual replay) | versioned per-symbol observation series |
| Shadow outcomes | new background worker | deduplicated rejected-candidate result |

Price-path capture is the largest hidden prerequisite in this design: direction labels, shadow
outcomes, MAE/MFE, exit scoring, and counterfactual replay all depend on persisted price
observations per symbol and horizon, and no such component exists today. Before any dependent
feature is scheduled, its design doc must bind: schema and uniqueness (symbol, source, timestamp,
path version); the price authority and symbol normalization rules; sampling cadence, gap handling,
and staleness thresholds; market-hours behavior for non-24/7 symbols; retention and estimated
storage growth; replay/version rules; and the labeling behavior when a horizon has missing
observations (labels degrade to `unresolved`, never interpolate silently). Until that component
ships, path-dependent labels use only the evidence embedded in the lifecycle itself (entry/exit
prices, recorded path extremes) and otherwise resolve to `unresolved`.

`tool-executor.ts` must stop persisting `execution_quality` cases directly. It records execution
facts and schedules finalization. `close_trade_finalizer.ts` becomes the only writer of lifecycle
execution-learning projections. `trade_policy_adaptation.ts` and `adaptive_decision_enforcer.ts`
must either be removed or called only as pure implementation helpers behind the canonical owners
(both have zero production/runtime importers today; their only importers are tests, so removal must
also migrate or retire those tests without treating test-only reachability as runtime wiring).

The gate itself currently embeds three unversioned journal-derived policies that this table does
not otherwise cover: the signal Sharpe filter (`summarizeSignalPerformance` over the latest 200
journals), `evaluateCalibrationSegmentPolicy`, and `evaluateDecisionQualitySegmentPolicy` (each
over the latest 500 journals). They block trades and multiply size from the same contaminated
labels and crowded windows this document quarantines. Each is either migrated onto the eligibility
service and the versioned champion/challenger contract, or removed; none may remain as an inline
recomputation. Without this, invariant 9 is unfalsifiable.

## Transaction and Idempotency Contract

Finalization uses `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` and an idempotency key of
`lifecycle_id + accounting_schema_version + label_schema_version`.

Accounting and label computation happen BEFORE the transaction opens, from an immutable read
snapshot; the locked transaction contains only validation and writes. Within one transaction it:

1. claims and validates the terminal event;
2. re-validates the read snapshot (event count and terminal-event identity unchanged);
3. upserts the canonical close;
4. upserts learning projections and prediction resolution;
5. writes policy evidence/audit rows;
6. writes counterfactual-work rows to a transactional outbox in the same database;
7. marks the finalization job row finalized IN the same transaction, then commits.

Job completion is part of the transaction (transactional outbox), not a post-commit step — there is
no crash window between "committed" and "marked finalized". Background workers consume the outbox
with at-least-once delivery and idempotent handlers. Shadow-outcome work belongs to rejected
candidates at gate-decision time, not to completed-close finalization, and is not enqueued here.

SQLite concurrency plan: `busy_timeout` of 5000ms on every connection, bounded finalizer retries
(the existing lease/attempt machinery) with jittered backoff on `SQLITE_BUSY`, and no network or
LLM call ever inside the transaction.

Retrying after any crash produces the same row IDs, evidence hash, PnL, and sample count. Policy
promotion is a separate transaction and cannot occur from a half-finalized close.

```text
learning.candidate.cooldownWindowMs         candidate_id dedup window bucket
learning.pricePath.observationIntervalMs    path recorder cadence
learning.pricePath.retentionDays            path storage retention
```

Exact names may be refined during implementation, but every threshold in this document maps to a
named key with a schema default; no magic numbers in code.

### Production-like validation

Add `scripts/validate_learning_lifecycle.ts` with two guarded modes:

```text
--temp-db                         destructive test lifecycle in an isolated DB
--production-paper --cleanup     uniquely tagged paper lifecycle with exact state restoration
```

The production mode must refuse to run unless execution mode is paper, snapshot all touched state,
use a unique marker, run one complete lifecycle and finalizer, verify every invariant, delete only
marker-linked rows, restore mutable aggregate state, and prove zero marker rows remain. It emits a
redacted JSON report containing release SHA, schema versions, row IDs, reconciliation deltas, policy
version before/after, and cleanup checks.

The production mode must also acquire a quiescence lock before touching state: pause autonomous
scans, the close finalizer worker, and background learning jobs (or verify the service is stopped),
and refuse to run while any of them is active. "Restore exact state" is not meaningful while
concurrent workers mutate the same database; the report records the quiescence method used.

## Acceptance Invariants

The invariants are scoped to the release that ships them (see Rollout): v2.6A owns 1-6, 13, 14;
v2.6B owns 7, 8, 15; v2.7 owns 9, 10, 16; v2.8 owns 11, 12. Each release fails if any of ITS
invariants is false on its exact promotion SHA; later-release invariants are not preconditions for
earlier releases.

1. Every new executed open has exactly one immutable entry context.
2. Every completed lifecycle has exactly one canonical close and one independent execution sample.
3. Net PnL agrees across all persisted consumers within declared precision.
4. Sum of entry fees allocated to close events equals actual entry fees exactly within precision.
5. Required entry context coverage is 100% for new eligible closes.
6. Direction, process compliance, invalidation, and profitability are independently stored.
7. No pre-cutoff, bootstrap, repaired, duplicate, or unreconciled row trains a policy.
8. Policy queries filter eligible resolved evidence before limiting rows.
9. A challenger cannot affect runtime before a promotion event.
10. Every promoted policy effect is visible in a later entry trace with its ID and version.
11. Repeated blocked attempts do not increase model rejection sample count.
12. Counterfactual evidence is reproducible from a path and fee-model version.
13. Paper and mocked-live lifecycle contracts have schema and semantic parity.
14. Finalizer retries do not duplicate closes, projections, weight observations, or promotions.
15. No journal-derived decision input reaches the entry gate outside a versioned, promoted policy.
16. No signal-weight challenger is trained on confidence-derived scores; weight training requires
    independently measured signal inputs captured at entry.

## Rollout — separately promotable releases

This document is the architecture contract for the program; it ships as four separately promotable
releases, each with its own acceptance-invariant subset (see Acceptance Invariants scoping), its
own release gates, and its own production check. A later release never blocks promotion of an
earlier one.

### v2.6A - canonical facts, accounting, migration

- Add schema, entry context writer with its state machine, shared close accounting, lifecycle
  identity and uniqueness, labels as pure modules, and migration.
- Dual-write old projections for compatibility, but mark only v2.6 canonical outcomes eligible.
- Run accounting/label/migration tests and the paper probe; no policy behavior changes.
- Owns invariants 1-6, 13, 14.

### v2.6B - single projection owner and eligibility

- Move execution-case and prediction projection into the finalizer transaction (transactional
  outbox).
- Stop direct execution-case persistence in `tool-executor.ts`.
- Ship the shared `LearningEligibility` service; rebuild eligible projections and quarantine
  ambiguous historical data.
- Migrate or remove the gate's inline journal-derived evaluators (Sharpe, calibration segment,
  decision-quality segment) onto eligible evidence.
- Owns invariants 7, 8, 15.

### v2.7 - labels in policy, champion/challenger

**Entry gate: at least 50 eligible v2.6 closes exist in the production database.** v2.6A/B are
unconditionally worth shipping; v2.7 is not started on calendar time. This codebase has already
produced three disconnected learning frameworks built ahead of their data
(`trade_policy_adaptation.ts`, `adaptive_decision_enforcer.ts`, the counterfactual tables); the
gate exists to prevent a fourth. v2.7 requires its own TDD binding the statistical thresholds
(effective-sample minimums, confidence margins, drawdown limits) left symbolic here.

- Change policy statistics to eligible net-R expectancy and hierarchical cohorts.
- Introduce champion/challenger promotion and rollback.
- Owns invariants 9, 10, 16.

### v2.8 - rejection and counterfactual learning

**Entry gate: v2.7 promoted at least one challenger, or explicitly reported that no cohort reached
minimum effective samples. Prerequisite: the price-path recorder design doc (see Runtime Ownership)
is approved and the recorder is shipped.**

- Add candidate deduplication, shadow outcomes, and path-based counterfactual replay (all on the
  shared price-path recorder).
- Remove stale/disconnected adaptive writers after call-site and data migration checks.
- Owns invariants 11, 12.

At the measured production rate (~76 executions per 30 days, 82 canonical closes lifetime), the
v2.7 gate is expected to take one to two months of paper trading to clear, and first organic
promotion may take a quarter. That is the system working as designed — the near-term deliverable
of v2.6A/B is evidence integrity, not visible adaptation.

## Definition of Done

Thufir is learning only when a completed, reconciled lifecycle produces one eligible observation; a
versioned challenger improves forward net expectancy; an explicit promotion activates it; and a
later autonomous decision records the promoted policy changing the outcome. Until all four links are
demonstrated together, the system is collecting and transforming data, not proving adaptive learning.

The four links must first be demonstrated together in the lifecycle integration test (test 7) on the
promotion SHA; demonstrating them on organic production data is gated on evidence volume and is
expected to lag the release by weeks to months. A release that ships correct facts, quarantines bad
history, and honestly reports "insufficient evidence to promote" has met this definition's v2.6A/B
obligations; it has not yet proven adaptive learning, and must not claim to.
