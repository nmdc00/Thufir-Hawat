# PRD: Opportunity Ranking Redesign — v2.3.6

## Problem

Thufir's current alert-driven origination path is too activity-centric.

What is true today:
- `TaSurface` can flag abnormal `oiDelta1hPct`, `fundingRatePct`, and `volumeVs24hAvgPct`.
- `OriginationTrigger` can wake the originator immediately when any `TaSnapshot` has `alertReason`.
- `selectDiscoveryMarkets(...)` already produces a mixed crypto and `XYZ:` universe.
- crypto and `XYZ:` candidates go through the same downstream risk checks, entry gate, and executor once a candidate exists.

What is not good enough:
- current alerts detect attention, not necessarily edge,
- `volume_spike` can dominate recent opportunity flow,
- abnormal activity is not normalized enough by symbol behavior or asset class,
- the system does not explicitly rank crowding quality, regime fit, or execution quality before originator focus,
- the product can therefore over-sample noisy tape and under-sample better but quieter setups.

The result is a system that can be directionally alive while still aiming its attention at the wrong symbols for the wrong reasons.

## Goal

Replace the current alert model with a ranked opportunity model that:
- treats activity as candidate generation, not final conviction,
- compares crypto and `XYZ:` opportunities under the same decision architecture,
- normalizes features by symbol and asset-class behavior,
- scores opportunities across multiple dimensions instead of a single spike trigger,
- passes a smaller, higher-quality set of candidates into the originator and discovery paths.

The redesign must preserve cross-asset parity:
- same framework for crypto and `XYZ:`,
- different baselines where market structure differs,
- no crypto-only or `XYZ:`-only logic branch in the opportunity model.

## Product Thesis

The best opportunities are not the loudest opportunities.

The redesign should reflect five ideas:
- `Adaptive Markets`: opportunity quality depends on regime, ecology, and participation structure.
- `Inefficient Markets`: anomalies matter only when they are tradable and comparable.
- `Beyond Greed and Fear`: crowding, overreaction, and underreaction should be modeled explicitly.
- `Narrative Economics`: attention and narrative spread matter, but they are inputs, not verdicts.
- `Thinking, Fast and Slow`: simple spike heuristics are useful alarms but weak final judgments.

The product should therefore promote opportunities that are:
- active enough to matter,
- structurally coherent,
- not already hopelessly crowded,
- compatible with the current regime,
- executable with clear invalidation and acceptable liquidity.

  - deterministic risk checks

  - execution

This means the redesign should not start at the entry gate.
It should start at candidate formation and opportunity ranking.

## Scope

### 1. Replace Binary Alerting with Ranked Opportunity Candidates

Current alert flags should become candidate-generation signals only.

The system should:
- still detect abnormal attention,
- but never treat a single spike metric as sufficient evidence of a high-quality opportunity,
- convert `TaSnapshot` output into a structured `OpportunityCandidate`,
- rank all candidates cross-sectionally before originator focus.

### 2. Introduce a Five-Dimension Opportunity Model

Every opportunity candidate must be scored on:
- `attentionScore`
- `structuralEdgeScore`
- `crowdingQualityScore`
- `regimeFitScore`
- `executionQualityScore`

Each component must be bounded to `0.0 - 1.0`.

### 3. Normalize by Symbol Behavior and Asset Class

The same scoring architecture must apply to crypto and `XYZ:`, but raw features must be normalized.

Examples:
- a `150%` volume spike should not mean the same thing on every symbol,
- funding extremes matter differently on crypto versus non-funding instruments,
- open-interest shifts should be interpreted relative to symbol-normal history,
- spread and liquidity expectations must use asset-aware floors.

### 4. Cross-Sectional Ranking Before LLM Focus

Originator attention is scarce.

Before the LLM originator or discovery proposal logic prioritizes symbols, the system must rank all candidates in the current scan and pass only the highest-quality subset forward.

This should reduce:
- overreaction to noisy single-symbol tape,
- inconsistent focus drift,
- hidden bias from whichever symbol happened to trip a simplistic threshold.

### 5. Preserve Shared Downstream Evaluation

After ranking produces candidates, downstream handling must remain shared:
- same risk checks,
- same wallet and budget logic,
- same entry gate,
- same executor.

The redesign should improve candidate quality without splitting runtime behavior by asset class.

## Opportunity Model

### 1. Attention Score

Purpose:
- detect dislocation or attention,
- wake the system up,
- never stand alone as trade quality.

Inputs:
- normalized volume spike
- normalized open-interest change
- funding extremity where applicable
- realized move abnormality
- narrative or event presence when available

Interpretation:
- high attention means something is happening,
- not that the setup is good.

### 2. Structural Edge Score

Purpose:
- estimate whether the setup has a coherent mechanism and attractive payoff.

Inputs:
- expected edge estimate
- directional structure / breakout or reversion quality
- cross-sectional relative strength or weakness
- signal agreement across available horizons
- freshness of catalyst or regime transition
- clarity of invalidation

Interpretation:
- this is the most important component,
- an opportunity should not rank highly without genuine structural edge.

### 3. Crowding Quality Score

Purpose:
- separate healthy participation from exhausted crowding.

Inputs:
- relationship between price move, OI move, and funding
- acceleration versus churn
- crowding / squeeze / exhaustion pattern labels
- evidence of one-sided positioning

Interpretation:
- not all participation is good participation,
- the system should reward constructive follow-through and penalize crowded late entries unless payoff is exceptional.

### 4. Regime Fit Score

Purpose:
- prevent the system from forcing the wrong setup into the wrong regime.

Inputs:
- classified market regime
- signal-class compatibility with that regime
- volatility state
- compression versus expansion
- trend persistence versus mean-reversion tendency

Interpretation:
- a breakout signal should score poorly in chop,
- a mean-reversion signal should score poorly in expansion trend.

### 5. Execution Quality Score

Purpose:
- reward opportunities that are actually tradeable.

Inputs:
- liquidity
- spread proxy
- stop clarity
- stop distance versus leverage
- target `R` quality
- book conflict / overlap / stacking pressure

Interpretation:
- even a strong thesis should rank down if execution quality is poor.

## Ranked Scoring Formula

Each candidate should receive:

```text
OpportunityScore =
  0.20 * AttentionScore +
  0.30 * StructuralEdgeScore +
  0.20 * CrowdingQualityScore +
  0.15 * RegimeFitScore +
  0.15 * ExecutionQualityScore
```

Design intent:
- `Attention` matters, but it does not dominate.
- `StructuralEdge` has the largest weight.
- `CrowdingQuality` is large enough to stop late, noisy chase behavior.
- `RegimeFit` and `ExecutionQuality` have real authority, not decorative authority.

## Hard Minimums

Candidates should be ineligible if they fail any of these floors:
- minimum liquidity floor
- minimum execution quality floor
- minimum structural edge floor
- minimum invalidation clarity floor
- minimum expected `R` floor
- symbol / portfolio conflict rules

A symbol can be highly interesting and still be ineligible.

## Candidate Generation Rules

The current spike rules remain useful, but their role changes.

They should be interpreted as:
- `attention triggers`, not `go trade` triggers.

Candidate generation should use:
- normalized OI abnormality,
- normalized funding abnormality where applicable,
- normalized volume abnormality,
- abnormal realized move,
- event / narrative shock,
- cadence-based scan inclusion.

No single trigger should force originator focus alone.

## Ranking and Flow Changes

Desired runtime flow:

```text
market selector
  -> TA / feature snapshot
  -> candidate generation
  -> opportunity scoring
  -> top-N ranked opportunities
  -> originator / discovery evaluation
  -> shared risk / gate / execution flow
```

Expected behavior changes:
- fewer noisy one-off alerts dominate the scan,
- more consistent cross-asset comparison,
- crypto and `XYZ:` compete in the same ranked slate,
- LLM attention is spent on top-ranked opportunities rather than whichever symbol had the loudest raw spike.

## Product Requirements

### Required Outputs

Each scan must persist or expose:
- candidate count
- ranked candidate list
- per-candidate component scores
- final `OpportunityScore`
- ineligibility reasons when a candidate fails hard floors
- selected top-N shortlist passed to originator / discovery

### Required Observability

We must be able to answer:
- which symbols became candidates and why,
- which symbols ranked highest and why,
- whether crypto and `XYZ:` were normalized comparably,
- whether a trade was ignored because it lacked attention, lacked edge, lacked regime fit, or failed execution quality.

### Required Cross-Asset Contract

The product is only correct if:
- crypto and `XYZ:` share the same opportunity architecture,
- normalization differs where needed,
- no asset class is silently advantaged by raw feature scale,
- ranking outputs can be audited by symbol class.

## Non-Goals

This release does not attempt to:
- replace the LLM entry gate,
- redesign wallet / budget controls,
- redesign execution sizing policy end to end,
- solve every possible narrative or macro inference problem,
- eliminate cadence scans.

The job of this release is to improve opportunity formation and ranking before those later seams.

## Success Criteria

`v2.3.6` is successful when all of the following are true:
- alerting no longer acts as a binary verdict layer,
- every candidate has a five-part opportunity score,
- top-ranked shortlists are available for both originator and discovery flows,
- crypto and `XYZ:` are ranked under the same architecture,
- ranking decisions are inspectable and explainable,
- tests prove normalization, floor behavior, ranking order, and cross-asset parity contracts.
